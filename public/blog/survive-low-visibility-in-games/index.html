How to Survive Darkness and Low Visibility in Games

Dark or foggy sections take away the thing runners depend on most: seeing ahead. Surviving them is about knowing which cues remain and trusting them.

Published 2026-11-16 · 4 min read

01

Find the cues that stay lit

Games rarely make the screen fully black; they keep a few cues visible so the section stays fair. In Shadow Shift, a black portal brings 8 seconds of darkness: buildings fade, fog closes in and the runner turns black, but the neon edges of the route and the obstacle colours stay lit. Follow those, not the buildings.

02

Shorten your focus

Normally you look far ahead. In the dark you cannot, so focus on the nearest visible cue and react to it. Decisions come later, so make them simple.

03

Trust the rhythm

The route does not change because you cannot see it. Gaps arrive at the same rhythm as before, so keep the jump timing you already have.

04

Stay calm and count

Low-visibility effects are usually short. Knowing the darkness ends in 8 seconds makes it easier to take one obstacle at a time.

05

Know the reward

Some games reward surviving the dark. In Shadow Shift, getting through it earns 5 seconds of auto-run, where the game plays for you.
